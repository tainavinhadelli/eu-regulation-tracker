    What this is

    An independent reference for tracking EU sustainability rules that are new, being phased in or being changed. It is modelled on the independent GHG Standards Tracker but covers EU product, packaging, green-claims, supply-chain and reporting law instead of US rules. It is not legal advice – always check the linked primary source before acting on a date.

    How the data is maintained

    The dashboard is updated with Claude (Anthropic's AI assistant) and reviewed by the maintainer before it is published. Each update follows the same steps:

    1. Search for developments since the last check for every rule and standard in scope.
    2. Verify every date, status and legal requirement against a primary source (list below). Where only a secondary source exists, the item is labelled as such.
    3. Record new developments in the Updates feed, newest first, each with a source link and what it means for companies.
    4. Adjust status, milestones, key points and confidence. Past milestones are marked done; things that were expected but did not happen (missed deadlines, delays) are recorded too.
    5. Check Austria: for every directive, the Austrian transposition is checked in RIS and the Parliament's database.
    6. Refresh the priority actions and remove items that are no longer current.

    Every entry is checked one by one at each update – including those where nothing changed. Each entry on the Regulations tab shows the date of its last check and what was checked.

    Entries marked Not yet verified have not yet been checked against a primary source.

    Source rules

    Only public information is used – nothing about any individual company. Every source link carries a label showing which of three tiers it belongs to. The source is always named, so readers can judge it themselves.

    Primary The official source itself. EU legal texts and institutions (EUR-Lex / Official Journal, European Commission, Council, European Parliament), EU bodies and agencies (EFRAG, ECHA, EEA), Austrian authorities and legal databases (RIS, Parliament, ministries, oesterreich.gv.at, WKO), and the standard setters (GHG Protocol, ISO). Required for any date, status or legal requirement wherever one exists.

    Secondary Professional analysis of the primary source. Law firms, Big Four and other audit firms, established consultancies, accredited certification bodies (e.g. DNV, SGS, TÜV) and recognised industry associations. Used for interpretation, context and early signals – e.g. what happened in a closed trilogue.

    Tertiary Reporting or summaries by others. Specialised trade press (e.g. EUWID, Packaging Europe, packaging journal), compliance-software and consultancy blogs (e.g. Coolset, Certivo, Terrascope, CDX, GPC Gateway) and other aggregators. Used only where nothing better exists yet, for example a leaked Commission agenda date. Items resting only on a tertiary source are treated as unconfirmed and get at most "Medium" confidence.

    Not used at all: forums, social media, AI-generated sites and sources that don't name where their information comes from.

    When sources disagree

    Both sources are named, the primary source is followed, and the more conservative reading is shown. The conflict is noted in the entry.

    Upgrading a source

    When a primary source becomes available for something first reported by a secondary or tertiary source (e.g. publication in the Official Journal), the primary link is added and the entry is re-rated.

    Austrian transposition

    EU regulations apply directly. EU directives only bind companies once Austria has transposed them – unless a deadline passes, in which case the EU text still shows what is coming. Austrian laws are checked in RIS (in force) and on the Parliament's website (drafts and bills).

    How to read the confidence ratings

    Each regulation's rating answers one question: how likely is it that what is described here is what companies will have to comply with?

    Confirmed Final published text or formally adopted decision, checked against the primary source.

    High Adopted or officially planned, but a date is conditional or a procedural step remains.

    Medium Outcome genuinely open – negotiations, consultations or court cases could change it – or so far only reported by secondary or tertiary sources.

    Low Early signal only, or the file is dormant.

    Dates shown as hollow diamonds on the timeline, or as "around" in the countdown, are plans or expectations and can still move.

    Limits

    This is an independent overview, not legal advice. It summarises; it does not replace the legal text. Before acting on a date or requirement, open the linked law text ("Read the law text" on each card) and check the current consolidated version.

    Scope

    In scope: EU regulations and directives on reporting, carbon pricing, energy efficiency, products, packaging, batteries, repair, waste and producer responsibility, chemicals, deforestation, forced labour, supply-chain due diligence and green claims that are new or changing, plus Austrian transposition where relevant and the Austrian Waste Management Act (AWG 2002). Also tracked: the EU voluntary reporting standard for smaller companies (VSME) and the voluntary standards that sustainability reporting depends on – the GHG Protocol and ISO 14001 – with other ISO standards listed for reference in the Regulation guide. Out of scope: non-EU rules.

    For voluntary standards the stages read differently: drafting, public consultation, final draft, published, transition period and required use.
